Why Free Software Matters

Free software is not about price. It is about freedom — the freedom to run a program, to study how it works, to change it, and to share it with others. That freedom is what turns software from something you merely rent into something you can actually own.

Free as in freedom

The word “free” is famously ambiguous in English. When we say free software we mean it in the sense of libre, not gratis — free as in speech, not (only) free as in beer. A piece of free software may well cost money. What makes it free is that it respects four basic freedoms: the freedom to use it for any purpose, to study and adapt it, to redistribute copies, and to distribute your improved versions to others.

Why it matters

When you can read the source, software stops being a black box. You can find out what it actually does with your data. You can fix the bug that is ruining your afternoon instead of filing a ticket into the void. You can keep a tool alive long after its original author has moved on, and you are never held hostage by a vendor who has decided that your workflow is no longer part of their roadmap.

Proprietary software asks you to trust. Free software lets you verify. For anything that matters — the systems we depend on, the tools we build our work upon — that difference is not a technicality. It is the whole game.

The awkward question: who pays?

Freedom and funding are often set up as opposites, as if the only way to make a living from software were to lock it down. We do not believe that. But we are also honest about the tension: good software takes real, sustained work, and that work has to be paid for somehow.

Our answer is not to go proprietary. It is to invite the people who can help to do so — not because a licence forces them to, but because they value the work and want to see it continue. You should be free to use our software regardless. If it earns its keep in your business, we hope you will choose to fund the freedom you are enjoying.

How this shapes what we build

This is exactly the model behind Perlang, the programming language backed by Xorway Solutions. Perlang is genuinely free software, released under the LGPL and MIT licences. Nobody has to pay to use it. But if you would like to help fund its development while keeping it free for everyone, there is a way to do that — and that is the point.

Free software matters because freedom matters. Keeping it funded without taking that freedom away is the problem worth solving, and it is the one we are trying to solve in the open.
